Same pantry, same bin, same stock: Gatorade powder at 18.3 / 48 oz, Peanut butter on a 3 month target, Oat milk on the household default with no rule picked yet. The second round sits between Fraction and Chips, and shows whether a target was calculated or typed.

Each direction replaces the sentences and text links with Tabler-style icons and 44px controls. The full sentence stays on the control as a tooltip. Icon-only buttons keep today’s accessible name (Rename, Remove or move …).

Current

What ships after #144. The bin already shows 18.3 oz and 48 oz, then a sentence says both numbers again. Rename, Change target, Change rule, and Remove or move are underlined text.

Fraction

The bin is only the picture and the name. On-hand and target become one tappable fraction: 18.3 is large because it is what’s in the jar, 48 oz is the quieter goal, and the yellow bar is the same stock color as the fill. The rule is a pill, “Same kind”. Rename is a pencil. A slash is the wrong shape for months, so Peanut butter and Oat milk keep short stacks instead of a fake fraction.

Tradeoff: “Same kind” is new vocabulary. The app’s name for that rule is “Same as what ran out”, and the sentence about buying the kind that ran out is only in the tooltip. This is the shortest page, and the one that asks someone to learn a pattern.

Chips

The bin keeps the numbers it already prints. 48 oz in the corner becomes the target button. 18.3 oz stays painted on the fill, because that number is “what’s in here,” not a setting. The rule uses the app’s existing name, “Same as what ran out”, as a pill, so the filter chips and the page say the same thing. Nothing new to memorize.

Tradeoff: less compression than Fraction, because the real rule name is five words. The desktop page is quiet beside the bin on purpose — that empty space is the deleted paragraph. The open menu below shows where “Remove or move” went.

Toolbar

Three square buttons, the shape of a Mantine button with a Tabler icon in front. Rename stays a word, so the only icon-only controls are the member menu and the barcode. The bin keeps 18.3 oz on the fill. 48 oz lives on the target button, not twice.

Tradeoff: more chrome than Fraction or Chips. “Same kind” is the short label again. If that feels like a new phrase, the button can say “Same as what ran out” and wrap under the other two on a phone. Easiest of the three to understand without reading a tooltip.

Member menu

Shared by all three directions. The kebab replaces the always-visible “Remove or move” link. Remove, and a move into Peanut butter or Oat milk, are each a 44px row. They show up only after the tap.

Between Chips and Fraction

CJ’s pass: keep the bin’s on-hand number, keep a rule name a person already knows, and don’t bring back the paragraph. These three are that density. What they add is the source of the target. Gatorade’s 48 oz is calculated (about 16 oz a month, kept for 3 months). Peanut butter’s 3 months was typed. Oat milk is still the household default, which is not a rate.

Auto is the only one with something folded away. The rate stays behind the tap. “You set” and “Default” are already the whole fact, so they sit in the row.

Badge

18.3 oz stays painted on the fill. 48 oz is the next control, not a corner copy and not a giant slash. Auto is its own pill. The rule drops one word from the filter: “Same as ran out”. A manual target has no pill. It says “You set 3 months”, in the same ink as the name.

Line

Same closed page as Badge, except Auto is a word and a chevron, not a pill. The rate opens in place, under the target, as one sentence. Nothing new draws a card. “You set 3 months” has no chevron: that row is finished.

Stamp

Closest to Chips. 48 oz stays in the bin corner, and the word “auto” lives inside that same button, so the source is on the number rather than beside it. The only thing next to the bin is the rule. Tapping the corner opens the rate underneath. Manual rows still say “You set”, because a corner on the little list mark cannot hold a sentence.
